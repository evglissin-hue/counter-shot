# Phase 2: a full match versus the AI (draft for review)

Goal: one player against an AI castle in a full 10 to 15 minute match. Both castles earn gold, build on plots, buy and upgrade weapons, repair, and train waves that march along routes physics can open and close. A match is won by toppling the enemy keep or storming its courtyard, and a guided first match teaches all of it. Every action that changes the match is an input tagged with its tick and runs in the deterministic sim, so phase 3 (online 1v1) inherits it unchanged.

Before phase 2 starts: both laptops play the phase 1 sandbox, and ideally the browser hash page is opened in Safari and Firefox.

Each milestone ends with the checks green, a commit and push, and a playable build at the same artifact link, with a short note to the owner on what to try.

## Layout

```
packages/
  core/      @keepfall/core     unchanged
  content/   @keepfall/content  + the map, buildings, minions, upgrades, the AI's plans, as data
  sim/       @keepfall/sim      + map terrain, bridges, the nav grid and flow fields, minion steering and combat
  rules/     @keepfall/rules    new: the match (gold, plots and buildings, upgrades, repairs, waves, orders, winning)
  ai/        @keepfall/ai       new: the AI player; it reads the match and sends inputs, like a person
apps/
  game/      @keepfall/game     the sandbox grows into the game: a start menu with Practice range, Versus AI and the guided match
```

- Direction: core <- content <- sim <- rules <- ai <- apps. The boundary check gets the two new packages, with the same determinism rules as sim.
- `rules` owns the match: whose stone is whose, gold, timers, what a building does, when a wave leaves, who has won. `sim` owns bodies and movement: masonry, projectiles, terrain, minion bodies, the nav grid. The split keeps the physics testable on its own, as now.
- The AI produces the same inputs a player does (fire with an exact aim, build, upgrade, order). It runs in the worker with its own Rng stream, so a match against it replays from its inputs like any other.
- The sandbox folder becomes `apps/game` in M1 and keeps the sandbox as its Practice range. The artifact link stays the same.

## Determinism and tests

- Same rules as phase 1: fixed 60 Hz ticks, detmath only, ordered iteration, inputs tagged with ticks, no wall-clock time. `RULES_VERSION` goes up whenever the same inputs play out differently.
- Flow fields use integer costs and a fixed-order queue, and recompute on a fixed tick schedule: a few fields per tick, the same on a fast machine and a slow one.
- Node tests pin the hash of a scripted match session per milestone; from M5, an AI against AI match replays to a pinned hash, and headless Chromium must match Node bit for bit (`npm run check:browser`).
- The lab's reference hash (35a7603f / 054e5eb0) stays valid throughout.

## Budget

Kat's laptop (Apple M3) is the floor: about 550 moving stones in 6 ms. Upright minions are kinematic capsules (cheap) and only become ragdolls when hit; ragdolls freeze once settled. With 60 to 100 minions per side at the peak, active ragdolls are capped (the oldest freeze early), and a match's worst moments (a wave under a collapsing tower) are timed on both laptops in M5.

## Milestones

### M1. The battlefield (open: a match page with a real valley)

- Map v2, from a seed and mirrored: a wider valley (about 160 m across, castles still 150 m apart) with a central road and two flank paths between low ridges, and a stream across the middle crossed by three timber bridges. Bridges are physics pieces like masonry: blow one and it falls into the stream.
- The sim's terrain follows the map (the height grid grows to cover it), and the renderer draws the same heights, the stream and the bridges.
- `@keepfall/rules`: a match wraps the sim with two castles and two teams, a match clock, keep income ticking up, the keep win (the banner falls) and an end screen.
- The nav grid: walkable cells from slope, water, intact bridge decks, standing masonry and settled rubble (step up to about 0.6 m), shown as a debug overlay.
- A start menu: Practice range (today's sandbox) and Versus AI (an empty enemy castle for now).
- Try: look over the new valley in the overview; drop a mortar shell on a bridge and watch it go into the stream (the overlay shows the route closing); topple the keep and get the victory screen.

### M2. Waves (open: minions march and fight)

- Barracks (pre-built in M2) send a wave every 25 seconds. Pikemen follow flow fields along their route to the enemy gate, keep spacing in loose ranks, step over small rubble and walk around big piles. Fields update as craters, rubble and blown bridges change the ground.
- Contact combat: pikemen fight when they meet; a blow knocks the loser down (a ragdoll), with odds per unit type from the seeded Rng. Pikemen at a closed gate batter it until its leaves come loose.
- Orders by hotkey for all your waves: Advance (Ctrl+1), Hold at the midfield marker (Ctrl+2), Push the breach (Ctrl+3, to the nearest walkable gap in the enemy walls). Each barracks has a route: left, centre or right.
- Unmanned weapons fire at minions on their own: slower and much less accurate than you.
- Try: watch the waves meet at the bridges; blow the centre bridge as a wave crosses and see the next one reroute; open a breach with the long gun and order Push the breach.

### M3. Gold and building (open: run a castle)

- Gold from the keep, Powder Mills, kill bounties, enemy masonry you bring down, and trick shots (ricochet kill, collapse kill, multi-kill, long-range keep hit), shown as they happen.
- Plots (6 to 8 in each courtyard) and the build wheel (B, or click a plot in the overview): Barracks, Powder Mill, Foundry, Signal Tower. Buildings are physics structures that rise over 10 to 20 seconds and stop working when knocked down.
- Emplacements (6 to 8 on walls and towers): buy a weapon for an empty slot, upgrade it at the Foundry. Upgrades are trade-offs (a heavier ball breaches more but drops faster; a rifled barrel is tighter but reloads slower).
- The rest of the Ashwright roster: sappers carry powder kegs to the walls and blow a breach (shoot the keg on the way and it blows up among their own wave), and musketeers shoot enemy minions from range. Each barracks sets its wave's mix.
- Try: build a second barracks and a mill; buy a second mortar; shoot a sapper's keg in the middle of its wave.

**Built in parallel with M4** (the owner's request, November 8), on its own branch, to be merged with M4. The detailed plan:

- **Gold** comes in through one income function (so sudden death can double it): the keep 5 a second; each working Powder Mill 1.5 a second; 4 for every enemy minion knocked down (by anything of yours: shots, pikemen, a burst, a fall); 1 for every 4 pieces of enemy masonry brought down (keep pieces count double). Kept modest on purpose (the owner's pacing note): M4's masons pay for every stone they put back, so a siege is attrition against the defender's gold, and more income would make castles harder to break; a match should run 10 to 15 minutes on sieges, repairs and storms, never on waiting. Trick shots, for shots you fire yourself: a ricochet kill (a ball that glanced off stone, then knocked a minion down) +15; a collapse kill (an enemy knocked down by his own castle's masonry falling) +10 each; a multi-kill (one shot, 3 or more down) +5 a man; a keep hit (your shot's first touch is the enemy keep) +10. The gold counter shows the rate (+7/s); earnings rise from where they happened as small brass numbers, a feed under the counter sums them up, and trick shots get a big toast in the display face with a brass ping and a jingle of coins.
- **Plots and the build wheel**: six plots in each courtyard, clear of the gate corridor and of where M4's masons and garrison stand: the two front plots where the old houses stood (6 by 4 m), and four side plots between the keep and the side walls (3.4 by 4 m or more). B opens the build wheel: a map of your courtyard with the plots numbered 1 to 6 (keys or clicks), then the buildings with their costs (keys 1 to 3); in the overview, click a plot. Barracks 220 gold (rises in 18 s), Powder Mill 160 (12 s), Foundry 250 (15 s). Each castle starts with its barracks on the front left plot. A building rises piece by piece from the ground up, with scaffold poles round it, the knock of mallets and a puff where each piece settles; it works while at least 60% of it stands and stops when knocked down (shell an enemy barracks and its waves stop); a ruined one is rebuilt for half its cost.
- **The buildings' looks**: the barracks is a timber-framed hall with the team's banner over the door and a rack of pikes; the Powder Mill has a stone ground floor, kegs stacked by the wall and a great millstone rolling round its post (a slow grinding rumble); the Foundry is stone with a tall stone chimney that smokes, a furnace glowing orange at its door and the ring of an anvil every few seconds. Chimneys and walls are real masonry: they come down like the rest.
- **Emplacements**: eight per castle, the four guns as now plus four empty slots (the front wall either side of the gate, the two back towers, and a second mortar pit behind the keep). In the build wheel: buy a long gun (220), chain shot (160) or swivel (120) for a wall or tower slot, a mortar (300) for the pit. With a working Foundry, each gun takes one upgrade (140), a trade-off: the long gun's heavy shot (a 20% bigger breach, 12% slower: it drops more) or rifled barrel (scatter cut to 40%, 30% slower reload); the mortar's heavy shell (a 20% bigger burst, a little less reach) or quick loader (reloads in 70% of the time, scatters 60% more); the chain shot's long chain (40% wider, weaker on stone) or heavy balls (50% harder on stone, slower); the swivel's canister (18 balls, a wider cone) or long barrel (a tighter cone, faster, slower reload). Keys 1 to 8 ride the guns; the aim help knows each gun's upgrade.
- **Sappers**: they carry a powder keg on the shoulder with a slow match glowing on it, make for the foot of the enemy front wall away from the gate, set the keg (its fuse sputters and hisses for 4 s) and run; the keg blows a breach you can walk through. Any ball, shell burst, grapeshot or falling masonry that knocks a sapper down on the way sets his keg off where he is, among his own wave (a trick shot waiting to happen). A planted keg goes off early in another burst.
- **Musketeers**: they stop at 15 to 25 m from enemy minions and fire (a crack, a flash and a puff of white smoke): a hit knocks the man down, more likely close in. Weak at close quarters. They shoot masons, garrison and climbers too.
- **Each barracks sets its wave's mix and route** in the build wheel: Pikemen (8 pikemen), Breach (6 and 2 sappers), Volley (5 and 3 musketeers) or Mixed (4, 2 and 2); a mix is data, so M4's ladder crew can join it. All working barracks send their waves together on the 25 s timer, formed up side by side outside the gate. L and the route chips set every barracks' route at once.
- **The enemy castle** follows a fixed build order in data until the AI (M5), paying like you: a Powder Mill at 0:45, a second barracks (Breach, down the flanks) at 2:00, a Foundry at 3:15, a swivel on a front wall slot at 4:00, and so on as its gold allows. Shoot its buildings to slow it.

- Repairs: masons rebuild destroyed sections for gold, bottom course first, wherever the space is clear; rubble is carried back into the wall. Auto-repair can be switched on per section (walls, towers, gate, keep).
- Storm the courtyard: your minions hold the enemy courtyard for 20 seconds while outnumbering the defenders inside. Sudden death at 15 minutes (income doubles, reloads speed up). Surrender. An end screen with what happened (gold, kills, masonry, trick shots).
- Ladder crews: they carry ladders to intact walls and climb over.
- Try: lose your gate and repair it under fire; storm the enemy courtyard with a big wave through a breach.

**The owner picked M4 before M3 (November 8).** Until M3, gold has one use (repairs) and comes only from the keep; trick shots, bounties and buildings arrive with M3, and the end screen gains trick shots then. The detailed plan:

- **Masons** (4 per castle, real figures with mallets): they walk from the yard by the workshop to the damage, clear the rubble out of the gap (carried off), and rebuild course by course from the bottom, one stone at a time each, paying for every stone (walls and towers 1 gold, the keep 2, a keep floor 10, a gate leaf 25). A stone goes back only where its place is clear (no rubble, bodies or minions in it) and what it rests on stands. A gin pole (an A-frame hoist taller than the wall, so it shows from outside) stands over the work; each stone rises on its rope and settles into its bed with a puff of mortar dust, to the tap of chisels and the knock of mallets. A gate leaf is hung back with a heavy thud. Masons drop their work and run from enemy minions close by, and they are as easy to knock down as anyone: shoot the enemy's masons to keep a breach open. A fallen mason is replaced at the yard after 20 s.
- **Repair switches** for your castle: Gate, Walls, Towers, Keep (Alt+1 to 4, or the chips in the match bar), all on to start. Masons take the most urgent job first: the gate, then the lowest gap in a wall, then towers, then the keep. The HUD says what they are doing and what it costs. The enemy castle repairs everything until the AI decides for itself (M5).
- **Garrison and storming**: each courtyard holds a garrison of 4 pikemen in front of the keep, topped up from the barracks. When the enemy is inside your courtyard, your barracks keeps its next wave inside to defend. Attackers storm a courtyard by outnumbering the defenders inside: a meter fills over 20 s while they do, holds while they do not, and drains when they are gone. A ring on the courtyard's ground fills in the attackers' colour; a war horn sounds when a storm begins, the bell of the stormed castle tolls faster as it fills, and drums build under it. Holding at the midfield markers until two waves have gathered, then pushing through a breach, is the way in.
- **Ladder crews**: from the second wave on, every other wave carries a ladder (two men with it on their shoulders: the shape reads at 100 m). They plant it in a crenel of an intact front wall (a thump against the stone) and the wave climbs: up the outside, over the wall walk, down a rope on the inside, a few at a time. A planted ladder is a physics body: a ball, a chain shot or a shell knocks it off the wall and everyone on it falls; so does masonry coming down under it. Shoot crews on the way in: once a ladder is up, your guns cannot reach the foot of your own wall.
- **Sudden death at 15:00**: three tolls of a bell and a drum roll; income doubles and every gun reloads in 60% of the time. The clock turns red.
- **Surrender** from the menu (with a confirm).
- **The end screen** says how it ended (the keep, a storm or a surrender) and when, with gold earned and spent on repairs, masonry brought down, lost and rebuilt, pikemen lost and knocked down, shots, waves and ladders, and a short log of the match's moments (gates broken, breaches, storms begun and beaten off, ladders planted, sudden death).

Findings for the owner (M1):

- The keep is still quicker to topple than a siege when the shots are good: in tests it took a long gun hit and two shells, two long gun hits near the top, or a shell and one low long gun hit (a lucky pair, 42 s into a match). ### M2. Waves (rules version 6, match rules version 2)

Built and published; waiting for the owner's verdict. What it does:

- **Barracks** (pre-built, one per castle): a wave of 8 pikemen every 25 seconds from 0:08, formed up in two ranks a few metres out from its own gate; a barracks sends only as many as keep its side at 96 standing. Your waves take the route you pick (L, or the chips in the match bar: left flank, centre road, right flank). Until the AI player (M5), the enemy's go down the centre, the left and the right in turn.
- **Flow fields** (`packages/sim/src/flow.ts`): for each team, a field per route and a breach field, over the valley floor (144 by 208 cells). Integer costs (5 a straight step, 7 a diagonal one, never past a corner) times a weight for being off the field's route (2 on it, up to 6 far off it), so a wave keeps to its route. In a route field the enemy gate is a door at extra cost (the waves who reach it batter it); in a breach field a standing gate is a wall. Dial's bucket queue, 2,500 cell visits per tick (about 0.2 ms on the cloud server once compiled), double buffered: a full refresh of all eight takes about 1.4 s. A field is rebuilt only when the nav grid's topology changes (a step between two neighbours opens or closes), not for every new heap.
- **Pikemen** (`packages/sim/src/army.ts`): they march on their field at 1.6 m/s (each up to 6% faster or slower), keep 0.95 m apart from anyone near, and keep their wave together (those ahead of its middle slow down). They walk on the nav grid's floors with eased steps: bridge decks, rubble, the fallen. When the floor drops out from under them (a deck blown away) they fall. They go for the nearest enemy within 9 m that they could walk straight to, close in, and strike within the pike's reach (2.6 m): a blow every 70 to 110 ticks, each knocking the enemy down with a chance of 30%, drawn from the army's seeded stream. Measured: 8 against 8 is over in about 6 s, and nobody is favoured by being spawned first. At a closed gate they batter it, pikes levelled: 60 blows a leaf, about 25 s for a full wave; the leaf comes loose and falls into the courtyard (the wall over the gate stays up). Inside they gather in the courtyard: storming it is M4.
- **Orders** (Ctrl+1, 2, 3, or the chips; all your waves, now and later): Advance (the default); Hold, at the midfield markers 4 m short of each bridge on your side (those already past walk back); Push the breach, to the nearest walkable gap in the enemy walls (as Advance while there is none). A wave whose bridge goes down before it crosses takes the nearest bridge still standing, all together (on a tie, its own left).
- **The nav grid**: rubble and the fallen are their own kind, a heap you scramble over (steps up to 1 m instead of 0.6 m; standing masonry keeps the stair rule); sandy in the overlay. Wreckage lying under the stream's surface leaves the cell wet, so a blown bridge does not bridge the water. A long gun shot high on a wall brings the top down but leaves a 3 m stub: a breach you can walk through takes a shot at the foot of the wall (about 1.3 degrees down from the gun, at the enemy wall).
- **The fallen** stay as floor: a frozen body raises its cells like rubble (ramps, heaps). Army ragdolls settle sooner than the Practice range's patrol (damped after 3 s, frozen once nearly still after 8 s). Past 240 bodies the oldest sink into the ground over 1.5 s and leave the world. A frozen body goes to the page once; the renderer keeps it.
- **Guns nobody rides** (`UNMANNED` in `packages/content/src/army.ts`, the match's gunnery): the four of each castle you are not riding fire on their own at enemy pikemen within 45 m of their own gate, the approach once the stream is crossed (the tower defence half). They turn at the weapon's own rates, lead the target, ignore the wind, and miss by up to 3 m plus a tenth of the range (the swivel 2 m plus 8%); reloads are 3 times the weapon's (the swivel 2.5). They hold fire when one of their own is near the target or a bridge lies on the line of fire. The mortar never fires on its own. Measured on an unopposed wave: 32 of 48 reach the gate (6 seeds), and a full wave breaks it half the time within 30 s of battering. You take a gun as it points: when you switch to one that has been firing, it swings back to where you last aimed at its own rate. Your trail and landing marker show only your own shots.
- **Gunnery** (`packages/sim/src/gunnery.ts`): the aim that lands a shot on a point in still air, by bisection on the elevation (the mortar: on the charge at a fixed elevation); the mortar lands within 0.4 m of the mark. The AI will use it too.
- **The enemy castle's guns**: its own four, your castle's turned through the middle of the field.
- **The page**: the match bar's second line (the next waves, the route and order chips, both sides' pikemen standing and down); notes when your gate is battered or falls and when the enemy's falls; the end screen counts pikemen; every gun turns where it aims; the midfield markers (a pennant in each team's colour on each route); pikes levelled in a fight with a thrust at each blow, and against a gate. `node tools/wave-shots.mjs` films a match on the real clock with fixed cameras; the renderer test page stages two fights and a gate for close-ups (`node tools/valley-shots.mjs fight gate`).
- **Determinism**: a scripted wave match (80 s: both barracks, a route change, Hold, Advance, a shot down the road, a breach in the enemy wall, Push, then every gun unmanned) is pinned in Node and replays bit for bit in headless Chromium, with the keep match and the Practice range session (whose hash did not change).
- **Cost** (cloud server, Node): the army adds about 0.15 ms a tick with 30 to 50 pikemen afield, with peaks of 1 to 4 ms when fields are rebuilt; the world's step grows from 2.2 ms to about 3.9 ms as bodies pile up to 110 (their physics, not the army's thinking). Not yet measured on the laptops.

Now they defend only their own approach, the mortar never fires by itself, and they miss far more; about two thirds of an unopposed wave reach the gate. ### M3. Gold and building (rules version 8, match rules version 3)

Built in parallel with M4, on its own branch (`claude/epic-hopper-0nsw4v-m3`), to be merged with M4 (both bump the rules versions once more then) and published with it; not published on its own. What it does:

- **Gold** (`packages/rules/src/economy.ts`; the numbers are `GOLD` and `BUILDINGS` in content): income comes through one function, `Economy.incomeRate` (the keep 5 a second, each working Powder Mill 1.5), paid every second in whole gold with the fraction carried; that is where sudden death doubles it after the merge. Every other earning goes through `Economy.earn`, with where it happened: 4 for every enemy minion that goes down, whatever put him down (the world now records why each man went down: a ball, a burst, masonry, a pike, a fall or a musket ball), and 1 for every 4 pieces of enemy masonry brought down (keep pieces count double; a building's count like a wall's).
- **Trick shots**, for shots you fire yourself (guns nobody rides earn the plain gold only): a ricochet kill +15 (a ball that glanced off stone, then knocked a man down), a collapse kill +10 a man (an enemy knocked down by his own castle's falling masonry), a multi-kill +5 a man (3 or more down to one shot, 4 to a swivel's and 6 to a mortar shell's, which downs most of a wave by design; counted once 0.75 s pass without another), a keep hit +10 (the shot's first touch is the enemy keep). A keg your shot sets off counts as your shot.
- **Seeing it come in**: brass numbers rise from where the gold was earned and fade, a feed under the gold counter sums up bounties and masonry, the counter shows the income rate, coins clink, and a trick shot gets a toast in the display face with a brass ping over a jingle of coins. The end screen adds gold earned and spent, and the trick shots.
- **Plots and buildings** (`packages/content/src/buildings.ts`, `packages/sim/src/structures.ts`): six plots in each courtyard, clear of the gate corridor and of where M4's masons, garrison and muster stand: the two front plots where the houses stood, and four beside the keep. Each castle starts with a barracks on its front left plot. Buildings are masonry like the castle (a barracks about 18 pieces, a Powder Mill 32, a Foundry 56): a barracks is a timber frame with plaster panels under a gabled roof; a Powder Mill has a stone ground floor in running bond, a plastered upper floor and a light roof; a Foundry is stone throughout, with a tall stone chimney. Every kind is laid out on every plot from the start and kept out of the world until built (the game layout grew to 10,303 pieces; the lab's is unchanged). A building rises piece by piece from the ground up (Barracks 220 gold in 18 s, Powder Mill 160 in 12 s, Foundry 250 in 15 s), each piece only where its place is clear: a body lying there is buried, rubble is carried off, the living are waited for. It works while 60% of it stands: a shell on the enemy's barracks stops its waves, on a mill its gold, on its Foundry its upgrades. A ruin is rebuilt for half the cost.
- **How a plot looks** (`apps/game/src/render/estate.ts`): stakes and a rope round an empty plot; scaffold poles while a building rises, each piece easing up into its bed with a puff of dust and a mallet's knock, then a heavy thud and a creak of timber when it is done. A working barracks flies its team's banner by its door, with a rack of pikes in front; a Powder Mill turns a drive wheel on its outer wall, with kegs stacked by the door and the low grind of its stones every few seconds; a Foundry's furnace door glows orange, its chimney smokes and its anvil rings. The roofs tell the trades apart from above: oak shingles on a barracks, light boards on a Powder Mill (built to lift off in a blast), sooty slate on a Foundry. Notes say when your buildings are up or wrecked and when the enemy builds or loses one.
- **The build wheel** (B, or click a plot in the overview; `apps/game/src/buildwheel.ts`): a map of your courtyard (the gate at the top, your left on the left) with the plots numbered 1 to 6 and the gun you ride as 7, then what that can take, with costs, by key or click: a building, a rebuild, a barracks' mix and route, a gun for an empty slot, an upgrade. Backspace goes back, B or Esc closes it; what you cannot afford says so.
- **Emplacements** (`packages/content/src/arsenal.ts`): eight per castle, the four guns as before and four empty slots (the front wall either side of the gate, the two back towers, a second mortar pit behind the keep). Buy a long gun (220), chain shot (160) or swivel (120) for a wall or tower slot, a mortar (300) for the pit; keys 1 to 8 ride them (an empty one says so, and a click opens the wheel on it). With a working Foundry each gun takes one upgrade (140), a trade-off: heavy shot (a 20% bigger breach, 12% slower) or a rifled barrel (scatter cut to 40%, reloads 30% slower) on the long gun; heavy shell (a 20% bigger burst, 7% slower) or quick loader (reloads in 70% of the time, scatters 60% more) on the mortar; long chain (40% wider, 15% weaker on stone) or heavy balls (50% harder on stone, 10% slower) on the chain shot; canister (18 balls, a cone 35% wider) or long barrel (a cone 40% tighter, 15% faster, reloads 25% slower) on the swivel. The sim, the aim help and the guns nobody rides all use the gun as upgraded.
- **Sappers** (`packages/sim/src/specialists.ts`): a powder keg on the shoulder; within 32 m of the enemy front wall each makes for its foot away from the gate and the towers, sets the keg (its fuse hisses and sparks for 4 s), and runs. The burst is packed into the foot of the wall: a gap about 3 m wide down to the ground, which the breach fields find. A sapper knocked down on the way sets his keg off where he lies, among his own wave (unless a pike put him down: then it drops unlit); a planted keg goes off early in another burst. In a 6 minute test match with only the guns nobody rides defending, the enemy's Breach waves set 3 kegs against your front wall and brought 73 pieces of it down by 6:00.
- **Musketeers**: when an enemy minion within 26 m can be seen (a line of sight over the nav grid's floors), they stop, aim for half a second and fire: a crack, a flash and a puff of white smoke. A hit knocks the man down, 50% likely within 6 m and 12% at 26 m; reloading takes 4.3 to 5.7 s. Closer than 3 m they jab with the musket, weakly.
- **Each barracks sets its wave's mix and route** in the wheel: Pikemen (8 pikemen), Breach (6 and 2 sappers), Volley (5 and 3 musketeers) or Mixed (4, 2 sappers and 2 musketeers). A mix is a list of units in data (`WAVE_MIXES`), so M4's ladder crew can join one. Every working barracks sends its wave on the 25 s timer, formed up side by side outside the gate; L and the route chips still set every barracks' route at once. Only pikes batter a gate (sappers and musketeers wait at it), so each mix has its use: in open-field clashes on the centre road (from both sides, 5 or 6 seeds) Volley beat Pikemen 7 times in 12, Mixed broke even and Breach lost 6 in 10, but Pikemen break a gate fastest and Breach goes through the wall.
- **The enemy castle** follows a build order in data (`ENEMY_PLAN`) until the AI, paying like you: rebuild what is ruined first, then a Powder Mill at 0:45, a second barracks (Breach, down your left flank) at 2:00, a Foundry at 3:15, a swivel on a front wall slot at 4:00, a second mill at 5:00, heavy shot on its long gun at 6:00, a chain shot on the other front wall slot at 7:00, a third barracks (Volley, down your right flank) at 8:00, each as soon as it can pay.
- **Determinism**: building, buying, upgrading, the plan, sappers' kegs and musketeers' shots are all decided in the sim from seeded streams of their own, so the old streams are untouched. A scripted estate match (110 s: a mill, a barracks set to Mixed on the right, a shot, a swivel bought, a Foundry, heavy shot, a second barracks set to Volley on the left) is pinned in Node and replays bit for bit in headless Chromium, with the keep match, the wave match and the Practice range session (all re-pinned: the layout grew). Unit numbers, minion frame slots, event kinds and the courtyard spots were agreed with M4 so the merge is mechanical.

Findings for the owner (M3):

- The enemy's buildings stand behind its walls, where the long gun cannot reach them (a ball that clears the front wall passes metres over their roofs): shelling its barracks, mills and Foundry is the mortar's job, or comes after a breach. Every mortar shell becomes a choice between a wave at your gate and the enemy's economy. Until the AI (M5) nobody fires on your buildings: the enemy's guns only defend its own walls, and its sappers' kegs blow the front wall, not what stands behind it.

The enemy's build order spends slower than it earns, so it banks gold (about 1,000 at 3:00 and 1,800 at 6:00 in a 9 minute test match); after the merge its masons spend it on repairs, and the AI (M5) will spend it better. With M4's masons paying for every stone they put back, more income makes castles harder to break; the merge will show how a siege plays. Say if buildings should cost more or less.

### M4. Repairs and storming (rules version 8, match rules version 3)

The owner picked M4 before M3 (November 8). M3 is built in parallel by another session (branch `claude/epic-hopper-0nsw4v-m3`), and a polish pass on the look, sound and interface by a third (`claude/epic-hopper-0nsw4v-polish`); they merge once each is done. What M4 does:

- **Masons** (`packages/sim/src/works.ts`, data in `packages/content/src/siege.ts`): four per castle, figures in the minion list (a ball or a blow knocks them down like anyone; a fallen mason is replaced at the yard by the workshop after 20 s). The sections: the gate, the front wall either side of it, the side walls, the back wall, the four towers and the keep with its floors (houses, banners and bridges are not rebuilt). A crew takes the most urgent section that can start now (the gate, then walls, towers and the keep, as switched on; the lowest gap first) and in it the nearest piece in its lowest courses whose supports stand and whose place is clear (a Rapier shape query over the piece's layout pose, a little smaller than the piece). Rubble in the place is carried off, or set back where it belongs if it is a piece of the same section whose own place is ready; fallen bodies are carried off (they sink away); a minion, a shot or a ladder in the place makes them wait. A section that must wait for another (wall stones resting on a broken tower) gives way to that one, and they come back to it. Each piece costs gold (walls, towers and battlements 1, the keep 2, a keep floor board 10, a gate leaf 25), and they wait when there is none. They run from enemy minions within 7 m, go back after 2 s with none within 10 m, and do not start on damage with the enemy within 9 m. Measured: after a powder burst at the foot of a wall (104 pieces down) the masons reach it in about 10 s from the yard and then put back or carry off about 1.4 pieces a second; the gap closes to walking about 20 s after they arrive, and the whole breach takes a couple of minutes and about 100 gold.
- **Repair switches** per team (`{ kind: "repair" }`; Alt+1 to 4 or the chips): Gate, Walls, Towers, Keep, all on to start. The enemy repairs everything until the AI decides for itself (M5).
- **The garrison** (`GARRISON`): four pikemen in front of each keep who fight only enemies inside their own courtyard and go back to their posts. A barracks tops its garrison up before each wave leaves. With the enemy inside its courtyard, a barracks musters its wave inside, beside the barracks house, to defend it; once the courtyard is clear the wave marches (or, shut in by its own standing gate, waits behind the garrison).
- **Storming** (`STORM`): attackers inside a courtyard (not on its walls or ladders), at least two and more than its defenders (masons do not count), fill its meter; it holds while they do not outnumber and drains twice as fast when none are inside. 20 s storms it and the other team wins. Measured: two waves through a breach overwhelm a garrison of four and storm the courtyard about 30 s after they set off from 30 m out.
- **Ladder crews** (`packages/sim/src/ladders.ts`, `LADDER`): from the second wave on, every other wave has two men carry a ladder (at 85% of a pikeman's pace, the back man in the front man's steps). Within 34 m of the enemy front wall they make for the nearest free crenel (four per front wall) where the wall stands to the top, raise the ladder in 2 s (the top swings up from the ground behind the foot) and plant it: a fixed body until a ball of 8 m/s and 150 kg m/s or more, a stone falling on it at 3 m/s, a burst nearby or the wall under its top giving way knocks it off (it turns dynamic and falls away from the wall). The wave, and anyone of its side within 24 m, climbs while the enemy gate stands and there is no other way in: up to four on the ladder 2 m apart at 1 m/s, across the wall walk at 1.4 m/s, down a rope inside at 2 m/s (about 14 s from foot to courtyard). Whoever is on it when it goes falls. Measured: an unopposed wave with a ladder plants it about 17 s after setting off from 30 m out and all eight get over; a garrison of four picks climbers off as they come down one by one.
- **Sudden death** at 15:00: income doubles (every source of income goes through one function, so M3's will double too) and every reload, manned or not, takes 60% of the time.
- **Surrender** (`{ kind: "surrender" }`, from the menu: press twice): the other team wins.
- **The page**: the match bar's third line (the repair chips, and what your masons are doing or why they wait), the storm meter under the match bar (who storms which courtyard, how many against how many, how long they have held it), the clock in red with a Sudden death badge, notes for ladders, storms begun and beaten off, gates broken and rehung, enemy masons at work and masons waiting for gold, and the end screen: how and when it ended, a side by side table (masonry down, stones rebuilt, gold earned and spent on repairs, pikemen lost, waves, ladders raised, storms begun, shots) and a log of the match's moments with the clock.
- **The look** (render only): masons in cloth caps in their side's colour and leather aprons, swinging wooden mallets onto a chisel; ladder crews with the ladder on their right shoulders, raising it with both hands, fighting with axes once it is up; climbers on the ladder and the rope with a pike slung across the back; ladders of weathered timber, and a rope down the inner face once the first man is over; a gin pole over the masons' work, taller than the wall so it shows from outside; each stone rises on the hoist's rope, swings over into its place and settles with a puff of mortar dust, and rubble leaves its heap in a puff; a ring on a stormed courtyard's ground fills round in the attackers' colour, bright while they outnumber the defenders and dim while they are held up.
- **The sound** (procedural): mallet knocks with the ring of the chisel; stones bedding in, floor boards, a gate leaf swung home with its iron settling; rubble scraped away; a ladder's top against the wall and its fall; a war horn when a storm begins (yours one long low note, the enemy's two horns beating against each other); the stormed castle's bell tolling quicker as its meter fills, and war drums building under any storm; three tolls and a drum roll at sudden death; drums and a low horn for a win, a deep bell for a loss.
- **Determinism**: the wave match now runs with both garrisons, both crews of masons, ladder crews and the repair switches, and is re-pinned with the keep match; the Practice range's session and the lab's reference hash are unchanged. New Node tests: masons (bottom course first, every stone paid for, waiting for gold, the switches, running from pikemen and going back), ladders (planted and climbed over; a ball knocks one down with a man on it), storming, the garrison and the inside muster, sudden death, surrender and the repair switches.
- **Looked at and listened to**: `node tools/siege-shots.mjs` (a breach, the enemy's masons and hoist, ladder crews, climbing, the HUD, a staged storm with its meter and ring, the end screen), close-ups on the renderer test page (`node tools/valley-shots.mjs ladders masons storm`), and every M4 sound in `node tools/sound-check.mjs` (clean, no clipping; the stone-setting sound sits low, since masons set a few stones a second).
- **Fixed on the way**: the enemy's guns stood on timber reaching down to the ground in front of its gate and towers (their mounts looked for the player's castle under them); masons rehung a gate as soon as the attackers in the gateway were down, and the next wave battered it straight down again (in one tour the enemy spent 675 gold on 27 gate leaves): now they leave a gate alone while enemy minions are within 22 m.
- **Published**: version 9 of the game link had M4 with the polish pass; version 10 has M3, M4 and the polish pass together (see below).

### M5. The AI castle, online play and a keep that takes a siege (rules version 10, match rules version 5)

The owner's go on November 8 (M3 and M4 shipped the same day), with three requests: two people should be able to play each other in this round; the keep fell in under 30 seconds (under five long gun shots and two shells), so matches were too short; and richer, more granular minion animation (done by a helper agent on its own branch, merged). Built and published; the choices below are mine, for the owner to confirm. Numbers are first pass and live in data.

- **The keep takes a siege** (`MATCH_KEEP` in content `castle.ts`; the lab's keep is unchanged):
  - 2 m walls of dressed stone (were 1.4 m): a blast reaches only half as far into them (`Stone.hard`), a ball that strikes them keeps less of its speed (it used to plough on and blast the far wall too), and their courses bridge twice the gap before the support check drops them.
  - Timber floors take two shells each: the first cracks a board (it stays, splinters fly), the second breaks it (`Stone.tough`). A ball punches through a board without bringing a floor down: shells break floors, balls strip walls, and a floor also falls when the walls carrying it go.
  - Shots pass the banner pole: a ball that clipped it used to knock it off the keep, which counted as toppled (a win in seconds, likely part of what the owner saw).
  - The mortar fires at 84 m/s (was 82), so the whole roof of the enemy keep is in reach in still air; a headwind still takes its back half away (the owner liked the keep at the edge of its reach).
  - Measured with a perfect gunner who fires both guns the moment they reload, with no repairs: from 1:18 (the pinned keep match: twenty long gun hits and seven shells) to about 3 minutes, depending on where the rubble falls (was 10 to 18 s). A mortar alone needs a dozen shells or more (rubble lying on a floor shields it), and against the enemy's masons it took 19 shells and 3:41.
- **The AI castle** (`packages/ai`, data in content `ai.ts`): a player in the sim, sending the same inputs you do and judged the same way.
